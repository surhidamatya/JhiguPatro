## Result (BS 1970–2100)

- This repo's table is identical to nepal_kit's for every year it covers.
- 17 years have at least one disagreement: **2062** and **2084–2099**.
- **2062:** only nepal-date-kit differs (months 1–2 are `31,31` vs `30,32` elsewhere). Three other sources agree with this repo.
- **2084–2099:** nepal-date-kit and nepali-date-converter agree with each other (through 2090) and disagree with this repo / nepal_kit / @remotemerge. In several years the two camps differ by a full day-count (for example 2084: 366 vs 365 days).
- **2087:** three-way split. nepal-date-kit sums to 367 days, which is implausible for a BS year.
- **2093:** @remotemerge also splits from this repo (months 9–10 `30,29` vs `29,30`).
- All five sources agree on BS 2000–2061 and 2063–2083.

## BS 2062, months 1 and 2: new evidence (2026-11-21), data NOT changed

The one disagreement in 2062 is months 1 and 2: this repo, nepal_kit, nepali-date-converter and @remotemerge have Baisakh 30 and Jestha 32 days; nepal-date-kit has 31 and 31. Month 3 (31) and the year total (365) are the same in both, so only the dates from AD 2005-05-14 to 2005-06-13 are affected.

A fifth, independent reading, from Hamro Patro's calendar pages (an unofficial but widely used Nepali calendar), fetched through a subagent that copied what the pages show:
- `https://www.hamropatro.com/en/calendar/2062/1`: last BS day listed is 31 (31 days); first day "1 Baishakh 2062, Thursday, Apr 14, 2005".
- `https://www.hamropatro.com/en/calendar/2062/2`: last BS day listed is 31 (31 days); first day "1 Jestha 2062, Sunday, May 15, 2005".
- Checked here: 14 April 2005 was a Thursday and 15 May 2005 a Sunday, and 14 April plus 31 days is 15 May, so the page is internally consistent. It agrees with nepal-date-kit (31, 31) and disagrees with this repo (which gives 1 Jestha 2062 = 2005-05-14, a Saturday).
- Limits of this evidence: one site, read once through a fetch tool whose output was truncated at the end (the month grids were complete); the pages do not show a full Gregorian date for BS day 31, so the month length comes from the last day number listed. A second site (vedicmarga.com) was tried and refused the request (HTTP 429). A search snippet that labels 30 April 2005 as 17 Baisakh is consistent with Baisakh 1 = 14 April but does not tell the two tables apart.

Standing: the four sources that agree with this repo may share one origin (copying is common in this ecosystem), so "3 against 1" is weaker than it looks, and the latest reading goes against this repo. **Still not resolved against the official Nepal Panchang Nirnayak Samiti calendar, so `data/nepali_calendar_data.json` is unchanged and BS 2062 remains unverified.** If the official calendar confirms 31/31, the fix is one line in the data (`[30,32,...]` to `[31,31,...]` for 2062), then `node tools/gen-data/gen.mjs`, the shared vectors and the every-day fingerprint (`testdata/calendar-all-days.json`) regenerated, and the tests rerun. No other test or vector depends on a date in the affected range; `testdata/calendar-golden.json` (the source comparison) is regenerated by `node tools/check-calendar/compare.mjs`.
